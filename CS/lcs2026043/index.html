Introduction

When I started college, I thought studying meant sitting at my desk for hours and reading the same pages again and again. After a few weeks, I realised that long hours do not always mean good results. I was tired, distracted, and still forgetting things before exams.

So I began to try small changes in my daily routine. Some of them worked really well, and some did not. In this blog, I share the habits that helped me the most. They are simple, free, and anyone can start them today.

The Five Habits

Habits That Helped Me

These are the five habits I follow now. You do not need to start all of them at once. Pick one, practise it for a week, and then add the next.

  1. Make a simple plan every night for the next day.
  2. Study in short sessions of 25 minutes, then take a 5 minute break.
  3. Keep your phone in another room while studying.
  4. Explain what you learned to a friend in your own words.
  5. Sleep for at least seven hours before an exam.

Things I Keep on My Desk

A clean desk helps the mind stay calm. Here is what I keep near me while I study:

  • A notebook and a pen
  • A bottle of water
  • A small timer or clock
  • A list of the topics I want to finish

Why Short Sessions Work

Our brain can focus well for a short time, but it becomes tired if we push it too long. When I used to study for three hours without stopping, I read many pages but remembered very little. With the 25 minute method, I stay fresh and can finish more work in less time. If you want to learn more about how to study and learn better, you can visit the Khan Academy website.

A Sample Study Schedule

Below is a simple schedule that I use on a normal college day. You can change the times to match your own classes.

My daily study plan
Time Activity Duration
5:00 PM Revise today's class notes 30 minutes
6:00 PM Practice coding or solve problems 60 minutes
8:00 PM Read the next chapter 45 minutes
9:30 PM Plan tomorrow and relax 20 minutes

This plan is not very strict. Some days I miss a slot, and that is fine. The goal is to stay regular, not perfect. If you are learning web development like me, the MDN Learn Web Development guide is a great place to practise.

Conclusion

Studying smarter is not about being a genius or working all night. It is about building small habits and repeating them every day. Planning ahead, taking breaks, avoiding distractions, teaching others, and sleeping well can make a big difference to your marks and your mood.

I am still learning and improving my routine every month. I hope this blog gives you a few ideas to try. Choose one habit today and see how it feels. Thank you for reading!
