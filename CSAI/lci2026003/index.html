Emotional Intelligence :

Emotional intelligence, an important skill that helps us understand our own emotions and recognize the feelings of others. Emotional intelligence, often called EQ, is the ability to recognize, understand, and manage emotions effectively. It is also about understanding ourselves, responding thoughtfully, and maintaining healthy relationships.
It helps us pause before reacting and choose a response that is more thoughtful and respectful.


The Five Key Elements

  • Self-awareness: Recognizing your emotions and understanding how they affect your actions.
  • Self-regulation: Managing emotional reactions and thinking before acting.
  • Motivation: Staying committed to goals even when progress feels difficult.
  • Empathy: Trying to understand other people's feelings and perspectives.
  • Social skills: Communicating clearly, understanding other's viewpoints, resolving disagreements, and cooperating with others.

My Experience :

Like in my case , it made me a person who could handle situations better than just letting my emotions take control of me. Understanding emotions does not mean hiding sadness, anger, or disappointment. Instead, it means recognizing these feelings and deciding how to respond constructively.
Understanding other people before making a wrong assumption because of the way they reacted or the actions they chose. It made me constantly think of their standpoint.

Being emotionally intelligent is a good skillset but it also has it’s own negative sides depending on individuals. Knowing and being completely aware of how your emotions affect you is a great thing. However, sometimes you find yourself searching for reasons not to empathise with the other person and end up reacting on instinct, even when you might be wrong in that situation.
In circumstances where the other person is driven by their emotions, you start to wonder, Why me? Why do I always have to be the one who understands them? Sometimes, I wish I could be the one to crash out. It becomes emotionally exhausting, and it is difficult to recover from that exhaustion. You reach a point where you stop feeling things the way you used to. You begin to feel the same in every situation, whether it is something you should be happy about or something that should annoy you. It feels as though you no longer care about your own needs, as if it is okay to be wronged, as if your feelings do not matter as much as everyone else's.


Conclusion :

It helps us face challenges with patience, communicate with kindness, and build stronger relationships. Learning to handle disappointment, celebrate others' success, accept mistakes, and express feelings respectfully can make our experiences more meaningful.
We do not need to change everything in one day. We can begin by listening a little more carefully, thinking before reacting, and trying to understand how others feel. Ultimately, emotional intelligence is a lifelong journey of learning, understanding, and becoming a more considerate version of ourselves.
