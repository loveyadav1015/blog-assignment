eSports in India: Growth, Challenges, and Global Scene

Introduction to eSports

eSports or electronic sports has changed a lot in recent years. What started as playing games for fun on phones and computers has now turned into a professional career for many young gamers. Big tournaments are organized with trophy cups, live streams, and cash prize money for winning teams.

While gaming is growing fast across the world, Indian players face unique struggles to build a stable life around esports. You can read more about esports standard gaming rules on the World Wide Web Consortium portal.

Rise and Fall of eSports in India

Mobile gaming became huge in India because smartphones were affordable. Millions of young players started playing games like PUBG Mobile and Free Fire every day. Local tournaments started giving out big prize pools, and gaming creators gained millions of subscribers on YouTube.

However, the industry suffered a huge setback when the government banned major titles like PUBG Mobile and Free Fire due to national security concerns. Teams lost their sponsors, player contracts got canceled, and the local scene fell sharply until new localized games like BGMI came back.

India vs. Global Gaming Scene

In India, most gamers play mobile battle royale games. But globally, PC titles like League of Legends, Valorant, and Counter-Strike 2 (CS 2) dominate the market. Professional PC players in League of Legends, Valorant, and CS 2 earn massive annual salaries running into millions of dollars through global league franchises.

What is the Esports World Cup (EWC)?

The Esports World Cup (EWC) is an annual global multi-genre esports tournament held in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. It brings together the top esports clubs and professional players from around the world to compete across multiple game titles simultaneously, featuring a massive combined prize pool of over $75 Million.

Challenges for Indian Gaming

Becoming a full-time gamer in India is not easy. Players face social, financial, and regulatory obstacles that make building a career very hard compared to players in other countries.

Key Issues Faced by Indian Players

  • Strict Parents: Most parents in India want their kids to focus only on studies or traditional jobs. They tell their children strictly not to waste time playing video games.
  • Low-End Devices: High-quality gaming PCs and flagship smartphones are very expensive, so most young players rely on cheap low-end devices that lag during games.
  • Lack of Infrastructure: India still lacks fast, low-ping internet in many smaller towns, along with proper training academies or bootcamps for esports.
  • High Government Tax (TDS): The Indian government levies a heavy 30% TDS tax rate on gaming prize money. This high tax deduction leaves players with much less income.

Main Steps for Overcoming Challenges

  1. Convince parents by earning from local community tournaments.
  2. Upgrade hardware devices after earning initial prize money.
  3. Join regional teams to get access to proper bootcamp practice spaces.

Indian Teams Performance in EWC 2026

The Esports World Cup (EWC) 2026 served as a major platform for Indian esports organizations to compete against top global clubs on an international stage. Top Indian organizations like GodLike Esports and S8UL Esports officially represented the country after joining the EWC Club Partner Program.

Indian teams participated across multiple game titles including PUBG Mobile, Honor of Kings, and FIFA/FC titles. GodLike Esports fielded rosters across 8 qualified tournament titles, while S8UL Esports competed in 6 titles. This global exposure helped Indian rosters gain valuable experience, earn international prize rewards, and establish India's growing presence in global esports leagues.

Conclusion

To conclude, Indian eSports faces tough challenges like high 30% TDS taxes, parental pressure, and low-end hardware. However, with game re-launches and impressive representation by Indian teams at EWC 2026, the future of gaming in India looks bright and full of potential.
